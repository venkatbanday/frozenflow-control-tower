### 5. How would you turn around a failing 3PL?
Establish a jointly agreed baseline and contractual definitions. Segment failures by site, route, customer and process. Agree a corrective action plan with named owners and dates, observe busy dispatch operations, and verify results on later orders. Escalate persistent failures to the MD and evaluate contractual remedies or alternative providers with a continuity plan.

### 6. What KPIs would you use?
OTIF, order accuracy, stock line accuracy, absolute stock variance, POD completeness, repeat incidents, action ageing, temperature excursions, cost per case and budget variance. I would define denominators, exclusions, source systems and targets with providers, then report trends and root causes rather than isolated percentages.

### 7. How would you resolve a shortage or over-delivery?
Contain the issue; compare SAP sales order, pick scan, dispatch manifest, delivered quantity and POD. Confirm whether the problem is order entry, picking, loading, transport or receipt. Agree customer recovery with Sales and supply evidence to Finance before credits or invoice adjustments. Verify subsequent orders.

### 8. How would you reconcile SAP and 3PL WMS?
Compare SKU, batch, site, stock status and transaction cut-off. Investigate missing or duplicate interfaces, receipts, picks, returns and adjustments. Use blind cycle counts and approved corrections; prevent recurrence through reconciliations and ownership. My SAP and WMS background supports this approach.

### 9. How would you manage expiry and batch traceability?
Use batch-level stock visibility, FEFO subject to Quality release and customer shelf-life requirements, and appropriate segregation. Test traceability from inbound records to stock and outbound customers, reconcile quantities, preserve evidence and work with Quality on incident or recall decisions.

### 10. What would you do during a temperature excursion?
Contain and isolate the affected stock; preserve temperature history, batch records and transport evidence; notify Quality; support impact assessment and follow approved disposition. I would not release product based solely on a single reading or an app threshold.

### 11. Do you have frozen-food experience?
My documented background is FMCG manufacturing, warehousing and logistics rather than specifically frozen distribution. I would bring transferable operational controls and work with Quality and specialist partners to validate the approved frozen-chain procedures. I would not present the prototype as proof of previous frozen-food experience.

### 12. How do you protect a key customer?
Agree immediate containment and a recovery plan with Sales. Assign one accountable incident owner, document the underlying cause, implement enhanced verification and monitor subsequent orders. Communicate progress transparently and close only after evidence of sustained recovery.

### 13. How do you manage logistics budgets?
Establish a cost baseline by provider, activity and volume. Validate invoices against agreed tariffs and activity evidence; separate volume effects from rate or efficiency changes. Review claims, prevent double counting, forecast variance and seek approved savings without compromising service or food safety.

### 17. How would you lead the internal logistics team?

My previous cross-functional team development involved 200+ people and a documented 40% productivity improvement. ### 18. How would you manage Procurement handovers?
Procurement retains inbound booking and supplier follow-up. Logistics owns outbound execution and 3PL operational performance. Agree booking, receipt discrepancy, stock availability and exception handovers, with clear timing and owners; avoid duplicated responsibility.

### 19. How would you tender or transition a provider?
Define demand, service specification, data exchange, food safety, continuity and commercial requirements. Compare total cost, capacity, capability and risk. Validate through due diligence, obtain approvals, agree transition milestones, stock reconciliation, pilot and fallback plans.
